# F16 confirmation horizon and sequential design

**Disposition for independent review.** Thirty sessions are insufficient at the modeled levels of serial dependence and economic effect. A longer fixed endpoint is the first design worth independent validation if a dependence model can be justified. The required duration depends on the smallest economically meaningful effect, the variability of the paired return differential and the complete comparison family. The release hold remains active.

## What limits confirmation

- **Mathematical impossibility under unrestricted assumptions.** Even with independent days and finite means, an unrestricted class permits a null distribution arbitrarily close to an alternative at any finite sample size. For an alternative with mean μ>0, replace an arbitrarily small fraction ε of its draws by a point at −(1−ε)μ/ε. The resulting null has mean zero and differs from the alternative on at most nε of coupled n-day samples. A test uniformly valid over this class has power at most α against that alternative as ε tends to zero. Under unrestricted temporal dependence, perfectly common shocks can also make a longer calendar equivalent to a single informative draw. Useful inference needs restrictions on tails and dependence.
- **Sample size under an identified model.** In a stationary Gaussian AR(1), with known correlation ρ and daily SD, the information for a constant mean in units of inverse daily variance is `[n(1−ρ)+2ρ]/(1+ρ)`. A one-sided Gaussian likelihood-ratio test gives the most powerful marginal test at campaign α for the simple zero versus positive shift. A per-comparison Holm decision, which requires a marginal p-value ≤ α even when other comparisons pass, cannot exceed this benchmark. At ρ=0.7 and 30 sessions the information is equivalent to 6.12 independent observations. Power for a 5 bp/day effect on 10 bp/day SD is **8.1%**, and for 10 bp/day it is **43.5%**. At ρ=0.3 the 10 bp/day bound is **92.5%**. The 30-session limitation is conditional on signal and dependence.
- **Invalid assumptions.** A longer campaign cannot correct a misspecified null distribution. At 240 sessions the Gaussian covariance test with a ρ≤0.8 envelope falsely rejected in **7.69%** of three-comparison global-null families when ρ=0.93. Scheduled looks gave **9.79%**. Both exceed the **0.4167%** campaign cap. Gaussian validity also requires a Gaussian joint law, including for the skewed volatility-clustering stress, which deliberately violates that assumption.
- **Implementation defects and statistical defects require different fixes.** The historical registration work addressed trade-date windowing and repeated validation. The v1 bootstrap implementation passed a literal block-index oracle check, yet its 30-session serial null still produced 25 false-positive families in 256 trials. The evidence shows a miscalibrated procedure in that design. It does not identify an arithmetic error as the cause. Increasing resamples reduces Monte Carlo noise in the bootstrap calculation while retaining the same 30 market observations.

## New synthetic results

The study generated 30,000 families per scenario. It covered stationary Gaussian AR(1), skewed shocks with persistent volatility states and dependence beyond the working envelope. It evaluated 3 or 12 paired comparisons under global nulls, one true null, half true nulls and all-positive shifts. Null comparisons have zero mean. Other comparisons in the mixed-null cases have a two-SD mean shift. Positive planning shifts are 0.5 or 1 daily SD, or 5 and 10 bp/day with a 10 bp/day marginal SD. The rates below are probabilities of **all** comparisons passing off a frozen policy, before the other certificate gates. The parameters are illustrative. No point-in-time market data justify these particular means, variances or correlations.

| True Gaussian ρ=0.7, fixed Gaussian covariance envelope ρ≤0.8 | 30 sessions | 120 sessions | 240 sessions | 480 sessions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 comparisons, 5 bp/day | 0.46% | 6.56% | 30.03% | 81.05% |
| 12 comparisons, 5 bp/day | 0.08% | 1.95% | 14.55% | 65.33% |
| 3 comparisons, 10 bp/day | 9.00% | 82.75% | 99.85% | 100% observed |
| 12 comparisons, 10 bp/day | 2.98% | 67.98% | 99.41% | 100% observed |

Using marginal misses of at most 0.20/m and a per-comparison cutoff of α/m gives a conservative **sufficient** horizon for 80% full-family power, regardless of cross-comparison dependence under the stated Gaussian model. At ρ=0.7 an oracle given the covariance needs 110 or 168 sessions for 10 bp/day with 3 or 12 comparisons. The ρ≤0.8 Gaussian covariance envelope needs **178 or 270** sessions at 10 bp/day and **723 or 1,093** at 5 bp/day. The envelope calculation uses its worst allowed daily mean variance. The empirical rows use the specified actual ρ and cross-comparison correlation, so their values can exceed the worst-case planning guarantee. None of these numbers constitutes an endpoint approval.

Some simulated Gaussian-boundary estimates exceeded α, including 163/30,000 false rejections with one true null at 240 sessions (0.5433%). This cell's binomial upper-tail probability at α is approximately 0.00063. The exact marginal one-null reference with the other comparisons passing is α. A separate draw of 500,000 correlated Gaussian prefix means using the dense covariance matrix gave 2,084/500,000 (0.4168%) at the fixed endpoint and 1,928/500,000 (0.3856%) with scheduled looks. Numerical integration of the three-comparison boundary global null gives 0.4131% at a fixed endpoint. Preserve the original estimates. The analytic reference and separate checks support the code and illustrate why a single pointwise interval near the boundary is an unreliable release decision across 650 inspected cells.

## Protocols for independent validation

**Longer fixed endpoints.** Keep the current cost-adjusted daily paired mean, full registered family and Holm correction. Plan the horizon before enrollment from the minimum useful effect, an external nuisance model or envelope, and a candidate-level power target. The joint distribution of the shared incumbent and other comparisons matters for planning. Counting trades or quitting after a near pass cannot substitute for this design. A several-hundred-session horizon also increases exposure to policy and market changes. It requires a credible stability argument and a rule making a broken policy, incompatible data or an assumption breach inconclusive.

**Conservative inference.** For a known daily SD bound s and upper lag covariance of s²rᵏ, the daily arithmetic mean has variance bounded by `s²[n+2 Σ(n−k)rᵏ]/n²`. A Gaussian tail is valid under joint Gaussianity and the envelope. Cantelli's inequality uses the same variance bound and needs only finite second moments, but had zero detections across the studied horizons. Using the large-n upper bound `s²(1+r)/(n(1−r))` and Cantelli on both null and miss tails gives sufficient horizons of 8,403 or 33,861 sessions at a one-SD effect for 3 or 12 comparisons under r=0.8. Those are very conservative sufficient bounds. The evidence does not establish a practical horizon for this rule. Truncating or winsorizing returns would change the target and would need its own prospective protocol and economic interpretation.

**Independently justified dependence models.** Gaussian GLS can be exact given an externally justified covariance shape and known scale, or with the t reference under the correct fixed shape and an unknown scalar variance. A plausible fit on the 30 confirmation observations is insufficient. Before enrollment, use separate provenance-tagged development observations from point-in-time paired policy replays to assess the common-mean assumption, autocovariance and tail behavior across regimes. Freeze either a justified model or a simultaneous nuisance confidence set and include parameter uncertainty in testing. For example, a supremum of valid p-values over a nuisance set with guaranteed coverage 1−γ, plus γ and capped at 1, is a valid p-value under that guarantee. Charge γ inside the comparison budget. An empirical interval that lacks coverage under future regime shifts cannot supply this guarantee. Composite families with implausible worst cases may be untestable in a useful horizon. Asymptotic HAC or a studentized dependent bootstrap would need tail calibration at the actual multiplicity-adjusted levels before use.

**Scheduled sequential inference.** The prototype allocates fractions 0.10, 0.20 and 0.70 to sessions 60, 120 and 240 of a *new* 240-session design. For comparison i at any scheduled look, take the running minimum of 1 and pᵢⱼ divided by the prespecified weight wⱼ. By the union bound over looks this is an anytime-valid p-value across the scheduled looks if each fixed-look p-value is super-uniform under the same null. Holm on these running minima controls the probability of any false rejection over the campaign. The running minima ensure that rejections are nested. This works with dependence across looks and across comparisons. A null about a common constant mean (or a null constraining every prefix) is essential. An average through session 240 that can be nonpositive despite a positive early mean cannot be tested this way for early promotion. Under the ρ=0.7 one-SD case, three comparisons gained **9.41%** full-family detection by day 60 and **61.80%** by day 120. The day-240 rate was **99.71%** versus **99.85%** for one fixed look. For 12 comparisons the respective rates were **3.32%, 43.57% and 99.09%**, versus **99.41%** at the fixed endpoint. Sequential scheduling adds a cost and only helps if earlier decisions matter and the common mean is defensible.

A likelihood-ratio e-process is another sequential option under an externally validated conditional innovation law. With exact AR whitening and a fixed positive mean shift, each marginal Gaussian likelihood ratio is a nonnegative supermartingale under a constant nonpositive mean. A prespecified mixture over positive shifts preserves this property. Inverting its running maximum gives a p-value to which Holm can apply. Estimating whitening parameters from the same monitored returns or swapping in a conditional bounded-return null changes the guarantee. The present study evaluated scheduled looks. An e-process still needs an independently justified conditional law and separate calibration.

These intervals describe Monte Carlo sampling only. No market observations or consumed holdouts entered the study.
- A second execution reproduced all three output files byte for byte. Checks verified the study and source digests, all 30 sample-size thresholds, 650 simulation rows and the monotonicity of scheduled rejections. The covariance formula agreed with dense calculations at the scheduled horizons. Sixty vectorized Holm results agreed with the application scalar implementation. These are implementation checks from this investigation.
- Calculate the smallest meaningful post-cost effect and justify the variance, dependence and stability bounds before choosing a fixed horizon or sequential design. Prefer the fixed-endpoint design as the first independent validation candidate. Its target and stopping rule are simpler. Proceed with a scheduled design only if immutable interim snapshots, restart behavior and the meaning of an early result are independently verified.
- Freeze a distinct protocol identity, source, candidate/control identities, full comparison family, exact eligible calendar, end date, null, evidence and power requirements before a new campaign starts. For sequential designs also freeze the look calendar, spending rule and whether an early statistical crossing is informational or part of the final decision. Define missing sessions, changes to model or execution and futility handling ahead of time. They must never create extra chances to pass. Preserve the α=0.05/12 cap and charge one of the 12 UTC-year slots at registration, including for multi-year campaigns. The annual bound then refers to the registration cohort. Decision-year error claims would require separate budgeting.
- An independent reviewer must verify the mathematical assumptions and implementation, then run a locked calibration on fresh streams with global and partial nulls, different effect configurations and realistic stress cases. Preassign the simulation precision and simultaneous assessment across release-critical cells. If the error or power target cannot be established, keep the result inconclusive. Final acceptance requires credible pre-enrollment model evidence and fresh prospective observations. The existing certificates and campaigns remain frozen and the F16 hold stays in force.
